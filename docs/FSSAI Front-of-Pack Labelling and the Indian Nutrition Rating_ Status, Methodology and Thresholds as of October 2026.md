# FSSAI Front-of-Pack Labelling and the Indian Nutrition Rating (INR): Status, Methodology and Thresholds (as of 21 October 2026)

The only official FSSAI legal text that sets out the Indian Nutrition Rating star system is still the **draft Food Safety and Standards (Labelling & Display) Amendment Regulations, 2022** (notified 13 September 2022). It was never finalised. FSSAI has since told the Supreme Court that it is moving away from star ratings to a **red-hexagon "HIGH SUGAR / HIGH SALT / HIGH FAT" warning label** based on ICMR-NIN 2024 thresholds.\[1\] The Court reserved its order on 28 September 2026, and no verdict has been reported as of 21 October 2026.\[2\] Your app's liquid values (30 kcal and 6 g sugar per 100 ml) **do match the 2022 INR draft exactly** (Schedule III, Table 1).\[3\] They do **not** match the thresholds FSSAI now proposes for liquids (70 kcal and 2 g *added* sugar per 100 ml).\[4\]

## TL;DR

- **Status:** The INR star rating exists only as an unfinalised 2022 draft (F. No. Std./SP-08/T(FoPNL-N-01), Gazette CG-DL-E-16092022-238893). In Supreme Court case 3S and Our Health Society v. Union of India, FSSAI proposed red-hexagon warning labels: two phases in its 28 August 2026 affidavit, changed to a single phase covering "any one" high nutrient in its 24 September 2026 affidavit. The Court reserved judgment on 28 September 2026 and said its order will bind FSSAI.\[5\] Nothing is mandatory yet.
- **Your values:** 30 kcal/100 ml and 6 g total sugars/100 ml are the official INR "baseline reference values" for non-dairy liquids. In the scoring tables they mark where a nutrient reaches 5 baseline points; they are not "high-in" cut-offs. The warning-label proposal instead uses ICMR-NIN liquid thresholds of 70 kcal, 2 g added sugar, 1.5 g added fat and 175 mg salt per 100 ml.
- **What to do:** Keep the INR engine, but label it clearly as a "2022 draft (not in force)". Build a second engine now for the ICMR-NIN/FSSAI warning thresholds, because that is where the regulation is heading. Watch for the Supreme Court order and the FSSAI draft regulation. According to ThePrint (24 Sep 2026), FSSAI's compliance affidavit said it "would need about four months to issue and finalise the draft regulations", followed by "a voluntary implementation period of 365 days by Food Business Operators (FBOs)".

**What this means:** The 2022 INR draft is not formally withdrawn, but in practice it has been superseded. FSSAI's own counsel told the Court that the 2022 exercise "concerned the INR star-rating system" and used "a completely different yardstick" from the warning-label scheme now under consideration.\[15\] Expect a new draft amendment to the Labelling & Display Regulations, with the shape and timing set by the Court's order.

### 2. INR methodology (2022 draft, Chapter 6, Reg. 14 and Schedules III–IV)

- **Basis:** per 100 g (solids) or per 100 ml (liquids), "as sold".\[3\]
- **Risk factors (baseline points):** energy, total sugars, saturated fat, sodium.\[16\] For liquids, only energy and total sugars are actually scored.\[3\]
- **Positive factors:** fruit & vegetables (FV); nuts, legumes & millets (NLM); dietary fibre; protein. Liquids score FV and protein only.\[3\]
- **Formula (Table 5):** Final INR score = baseline points − (FV + NLM + protein + fibre points).\[3\]
- **Categories:** Category I solids, which includes all food categories except 6.8.1 and 14.0 (Table 2 is oddly titled "Solid Foods (Incl. Dairy products and beverages)"). Category II liquids are food categories 6.8.1 and 14.0, excluding dairy. Category III is exempt (Schedule IV).\[3\]\[16\]
- **Positive-point caps (Table 4):**
  - Solids with baseline ≤ 20: up to 15 points for protein and 10 each for FV, NLM and fibre. Baseline > 20: 7 for protein, 5 each for the others.\[3\]
  - Liquids with baseline ≤ 10: up to 10 for FV and 15 for protein. Baseline > 10: 5 for FV and 7 for protein.\[3\]
- **Display:** the logo sits close to the product or brand name. Optional per-serve %RDA for energy, sugars, saturated fat and salt can be shown at 25% of logo size. Minimum logo size is 15/20/25 mm for principal display panels of >100–500, >500–2500 and >2500 cm². The logo is generated through FoSCoS.\[3\]

### 3. Your app's liquid values: verdict

- **They match the 2022 INR draft.** Schedule III, Table 1 of the official Gazette PDF (page 39 of the 44272/2022 file numbering) lists energy at 30 kcal and total sugars at 6 g for "Liquid Foods (non-dairy)".\[3\]
- **How the draft uses them:** they are *reference anchors*, not pass/fail limits. In Table 3, >30 kcal scores 5 energy points and >6.1 g sugar scores 5 sugar points, which is half of the maximum 10.\[3\] Each point step is one-fifth of the reference value: 6 kcal for energy, and about 1.5 g for sugar, offset by 0.1 g. If your app treats 30 kcal and 6 g as "high-in" cut-offs, that is not how the draft uses them. Score with Table 3 instead.
- **They do not match the current proposal.** The ICMR-NIN DGI 2024 Table 15.1, which FSSAI's affidavits rely on and the 10 September 2026 order quotes, gives for liquids: **70 kcal, 2 g added sugar, 1.5 g added fat, 175 mg salt per 100 ml**. For solids it gives 250 kcal, 3 g added sugar, 4.2 g added fat and 625 mg salt per 100 g.\[4\]\[13\]\[17\] The official school-food draft of August 2026 uses the same fat, sugar and salt figures but no energy figure.\[12\]\[18\] Note that this table uses *added* sugar, not total sugar, and *salt*, not sodium.

### 4. Warning labels vs star rating, and exemptions

- **2022 draft:** no warning label at all. It has the star rating plus an HFSS *definition*: a product is HFSS if energy from total sugar is not below 10% of total energy, energy from saturated fat is not below 10%, and sodium is not below 1 mg/kcal. Products carrying the milk logo are exempt from the HFSS definition.\[3\]
- **2022 exemptions:** all foods exempt from nutrition labelling. Any beverage or carbonated beverage "without energy and/or sugar" cannot declare an INR. Schedule IV (Category III) exempts plain milk, curd and buttermilk, cream, milk powders, whey, oils and fats and ghee, fresh and frozen fruit and vegetables, whole grains, flours and starches, batters, fresh meat, fish and eggs, sugars, honey and table-top sweeteners, salt, spices and condiments, vinegar, yeast, infant and special-medical foods, food supplements, waters, and all alcoholic beverages.\[3\]\[16\]
- **2026 proposal (not yet official text):** a red hexagon on a white square with HIGH FAT, HIGH SUGAR, HIGH SALT or HIGHLY SWEETENED BEVERAGE. Font is one point larger than the back-of-pack nutrition table. There is a separate "CONTAIN NON CALORIC SWEETENER" label. Proposed exemptions are single-ingredient foods and foods inherently high in fat, sugar or salt (ghee, edible oil, salt, sugar, jaggery, honey).\[18\]\[19\]\[20\] The covered sweetened-beverage categories reported for the 24 September affidavit are carbonated and non-carbonated water-based drinks, fruit drinks, syrups and sharbats, and caffeinated drinks.\[1\]\[4\]\[21\] No separate beverage threshold has been reported.

## Recommendations

1. **Label your current engine "INR – 2022 draft, not in force".** The 30 kcal and 6 g values are correct for that draft. Score with Table 3 points rather than treating the values as binary limits. Apply the Table 4 caps and the liquid Table 6 bands, handling the 18/20 overlap explicitly, for example by assigning 18 → 1.5★ and 20 → 1★.
2. **Add a "warning-label" mode now** using the ICMR-NIN/FSSAI school-draft thresholds. For liquids: added sugar > 2 g, added fat > 1.5 g, salt > 0.175 g per 100 ml. For solids: > 3 g, > 4.2 g, > 0.625 g per 100 g.\[22\] Store "added" and "total" sugar separately, because the Court has asked FSSAI to justify using added rather than total sugar.\[4\]\[13\] Convert sodium to salt using ×2.5.
3. **Make thresholds configurable.** The Supreme Court order and the follow-on FSSAI draft could change the trigger, the nutrient basis (added vs total, saturated vs added fat) or add processing-level thresholds.\[13\]
4. **Monitor** the Supreme Court's pronouncement in MA 1177/2025 in W.P.(C) 437/2024 and the FSSAI notifications page for a new Labelling & Display amendment.

## Caveats

- The Supreme Court material (affidavits, the 10 Sep order) was seen only through news and law-report sites, not sci.gov.in. The affidavit contents are reported secondhand.
- The 2022 draft has internal oddities. Liquid saturated fat (3 g) and sodium (100 mg) appear in Table 1 but are not scored in Table 3. The liquid star bands overlap at 18 and 20. Category II is defined by codes 6.8.1 and 14.0, while 14.x waters and alcohol are separately exempt.
- The ICMR-NIN 70 kcal liquid energy threshold appears in the DGI and the affidavits but **not** in the school-food draft regulation.\[12\]
- A Gazette ID (CG-DL-E-16092022-238893) is given for the 2022 draft, but no egazette.gov.in URL was verified. Use the fssai.gov.in copy of the Gazette.
